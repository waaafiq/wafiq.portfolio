ERICA Research · Campus wayfinding

Why students get lost at ERICA, and what a map app should do about it

A survey of 45 Hanyang ERICA students showed that the hardest part of getting to class is not finding the building. It is finding the room once you are inside. And the most popular map app works worst for students who don't read Korean.

The question

What makes it hard to find your way around ERICA, and does knowing Korean change that?

ERICA timetables list classes only as a building number and a room number. The popular map apps are built for streets, not campuses. We wanted to know where exactly students lose their way, and whether international students struggle more than Korean students.

Findings

Finding 1

Finding the room is as hard as or harder than finding the building

Trouble finding a buildingTrouble finding a room inside it

Even native Korean speakers, who rarely got lost between buildings, struggled once inside (33% vs 60%). Building-level difficulty rose as Korean ability fell (33% to 67%), but that trend was not statistically significant with this sample.

Rooms: binomial p = 0.036 · Buildings by group: χ²(2, N = 45) = 3.379, p = 0.185

Finding 2

The most-used map app works far less well for students who don't read Korean

58% of students use Naver Map on campus. Among them, native speakers rated it 4.4 out of 5, while students with no Korean rated it 2.8. The official Hanyang campus map scored about the same across groups (3.8 to 4.0), which suggests the gap comes from the app rather than the students.

One-way ANOVA with Scheffé post hoc · native vs some Korean p = 0.028 · native vs no Korean p = 0.001 · some vs no Korean p = 0.151

Finding 3

Building numbers are the main source of confusion

The two most common problems were both about building numbers: 56% could not search for a building by its number, and 49% could not match the number on their timetable to a building on the map. Campus signs did not fill the gap: only 15 of 45 students rated them clear.

Multi-select, share of 45 respondents · Sign clarity: 15 rated clear or very clear, 21 neutral, 9 unclear

Finding 4

The problem peaks in the first weeks of the semester

Almost every student (89%) said navigation was hardest in the first weeks of the semester, and 71% said it was hard when visiting a building for the first time. Students also started with low familiarity: in their first one to two months, the average rating was below 2 out of 5 in every group.

Multi-select, share of 45 respondents · First-months familiarity (1 to 5): native 1.87, some Korean 1.60, no Korean 1.47

Reflection

What I would do differently

  • Recruit more participants. Some subgroups were very small, which limits how far the ANOVA result can be trusted.
  • Measure behaviour, not only opinions. A timed task, such as walking from the main gate to a specific room, would show real navigation time instead of self-reported difficulty.
  • Test against a baseline. The prototype was rated on its own, not side by side with Naver Map or the official campus map.
  • Ask about indoor navigation in more detail. It turned out to be the biggest problem, but the survey had only two questions on it. That is also why indoor maps are the next thing I am building: the data says it matters most, and a follow-up study will show which buildings to map first.
